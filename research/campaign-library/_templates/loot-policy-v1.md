# DungeonMasterOS Campaign Loot Policy v1

Status: central campaign-gate policy
Date: 2026-08-17

## Purpose

Every built-in DungeonMasterOS campaign must provide tangible discovery rewards in addition to monsters, traps, dialogue and plot progression. A campaign that contains no meaningful items, treasure, salvage, consumables, documents, equipment or other recoverable resources fails the campaign gate even if its story graph is otherwise complete.

The goal is not to turn every creature into a loot pinata. Rewards must make sense in the fiction, preserve the source adventure, respect the native ruleset, and remain persistent campaign state.

## 1. Source loot always wins

1. Preserve every source-defined item, treasure cache, carried possession, shop stock, salvage object, quest reward and random treasure procedure that matters to play.
2. Preserve source ownership. Finding an item does not automatically mean the party may take it without consequences.
3. Preserve source rarity, mechanics, charges, traps, conditions and placement unless an explicit rules adapter is active.
4. Never replace a source item with a DungeonMasterOS item merely because the master compendium contains something superficially similar.
5. Source-defined random tables remain source-defined random tables. Do not cherry-pick favourable results.
6. Campaign-unique source items should receive source-aware definition keys when the compendium integration supports them.

## 2. Minimum discovery density

Every campaign must declare a loot profile and enough tangible reward beats for its scale.

- `micro` scenario: at least 2 tangible reward beats.
- `one-shot`: at least 3 tangible reward beats.
- `short` adventure / roughly one to two sessions: at least 4 tangible reward beats.
- `multi-session`: at least 6 tangible reward beats, then additional rewards distributed across major regions/acts as play expands.

At least one reward should be useful rather than purely monetary when the source and ruleset permit it. A memorable/signature item, relic, unusual tool, document, salvage object or meaningful quest reward is strongly preferred in every non-micro campaign.

A `reward beat` is a meaningful opportunity to obtain or interact with something tangible. Twenty identical copper purses on twenty identical goblins do not count as twenty meaningful beats.

## 3. Supplemental DungeonMasterOS loot

Supplemental loot is allowed only when the source is genuinely sparse or explicitly leaves a reward/result to the GM. It is a separate provenance layer and must never be represented internally as source canon.

Allowed modes:

- `none`: source already has sufficient loot.
- `gap-fill`: DMOS may add sparse, plausible discoveries to prevent long reward droughts.
- `source-authorized-random`: the source explicitly tells the GM to choose/roll an unspecified item or treasure.
- `native-table`: the source/ruleset expects normal native treasure tables in specified areas.

Supplemental item selection order:

1. exact native-ruleset/source definition;
2. approved rules-adapter definition;
3. compatible DungeonMasterOS compendium definition;
4. campaign-scoped homebrew definition if explicitly reviewed.

Do not drop a D&D 5e item into Cairn, Basic Fantasy, Shadowdark or another system just because it exists in `item_definitions`. The selected rules adapter must approve the mechanics/power band first.

## 4. Default supplemental opportunity weights

These percentages apply only when a location/encounter has already qualified as a **supplemental loot opportunity**. They are not rolled for every monster or every room.

- 55% mundane value, coin, salvage, ammunition, material or ordinary gear
- 30% consumable, limited-use resource, useful tool, scroll/document or utility item
- 12% minor magical/special item appropriate to the party's current power band
- 2.5% notable magical/special item appropriate to the party's current power band
- 0.5% exceptional jackpot item, subject to the power cap below

Artifacts, campaign-defining relics and equivalent unique items are **never** generated by this random table. They must be authored/source-backed rewards.

Campaigns may override the weights only when their native ruleset/economy calls for it and the override is recorded in `loot-profile.json`.

## 5. Power-band caps

For level-based fantasy adapters, the default random-loot ceiling is:

- Levels 1-4: ordinary/minor items normally; rare-equivalent only as the exceptional jackpot. No random very-rare/legendary-equivalent rewards.
- Levels 5-10: minor/notable items normally; very-rare-equivalent only as an exceptional jackpot.
- Levels 11-16: notable/high-tier rewards may appear; legendary-equivalent remains extremely rare and context-dependent.
- Levels 17+: high-tier rewards may appear when appropriate, but artifacts/unique campaign relics remain authored only.

For Cairn, Shadowdark, Mork Borg, Basic Fantasy, Mothership, FIST, system-neutral adventures and other non-5e models, the selected rules adapter maps these concepts to the native power/economy model. Never force D&D rarity vocabulary into the player-facing game when the native system does not use it.

## 6. Placement plausibility

Prefer these placement types:

- carried/worn by an NPC or intelligent enemy;
- hidden cache or chest;
- locked/trapped container;
- corpse or previous expedition remains;
- environmental salvage;
- lair/hoard;
- quest or faction reward;
- merchant/trade stock;
- document/map/key/clue with practical value;
- crafting component or monster part when the ruleset supports it.

Creature rules:

- Intelligent humanoids may carry money, tools, weapons, keys, consumables or personal objects when plausible.
- Named enemies and bosses should usually have at least one distinctive recoverable possession if the source/campaign context permits it.
- Ordinary beasts do not have pockets. Valuable material may instead be in a nest, stomach, prior victim remains, hide, venom, horn, gland or other contextually sensible source.
- Undead may retain burial goods, weapons, jewellery, ritual objects or possessions from life.
- Constructs may yield components, cores, tools or salvage rather than biological loot.

## 7. Anti-farm and persistence rules

All random/supplemental loot is campaign state.

- Generate a qualifying cache/drop once from stable campaign/location/encounter state.
- Persist the result before or when it is revealed.
- Revisiting the room does not reroll it.
- Reloading/retrying does not reroll it.
- Dead enemies do not respawn their valuables unless the fiction actually introduces new occupants.
- Unique items cannot be duplicated by repeated generation.

## 8. Master compendium integration

The draft `feature/master-item-compendium` branch provides `definitionKey`, ruleset, edition, category, rarity, consumable/equippable flags, mechanics, effects and provenance fields. Campaign loot should eventually reference those definition keys when a compatible definition exists.

Until that branch is reconciled with Claude's current Inventory/Use/Read/Equip work:

- campaign research may record intended definition keys;
- source-unique items may remain campaign-scoped definitions;
- no research branch should overwrite or merge Claude's VPS inventory files;
- compendium selection must filter by ruleset/adapter and power band before rarity or thematic preference.

## 9. Approval gate

A campaign cannot be centrally approved unless:

1. a valid `loot-profile.json` exists;
2. source-defined loot has been audited and preserved;
3. the campaign meets the minimum tangible reward density for its scale;
4. at least one useful/non-currency reward exists when appropriate to the source;
5. any supplemental loot mode is explicitly declared;
6. supplemental rewards are ruleset/power-band compatible;
7. random loot is persistent and non-farmable;
8. source versus DMOS-added provenance remains distinguishable internally.

This gate applies to existing approved campaigns as they are re-audited and to every future campaign before approval.
