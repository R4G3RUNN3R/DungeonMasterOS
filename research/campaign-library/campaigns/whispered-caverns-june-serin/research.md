# The Whispered Caverns of June Serin - Source-derived research

Only facts exposed by the primary creator page are hard source facts at this stage.

## Opening mystery

Mining crews working in the lowest reaches of the Whispered Caverns return from whole shifts with no memory of what happened during those hours. Despite that, they bring back three or four times their normal quantities of metal ore, and the ore is suspiciously pure.

Landry Horn, an old friend of the player characters, travelled into the Frontier Lands seeking a fortune and ended up working as a miner in June Serin. Landry sends the party a coded letter pleading for help. The party chooses to answer the call.

## Source-defined play emphasis

The creator rates the adventure approximately:
- Exploration: 3/5
- Social: 4/5
- Combat: 2/5

The page explicitly says investigation/problem solving occurs through environmental clues and social interactions, with some combat. This establishes that clues and NPC conversations are core progression mechanics, not optional flavour.

## Character assumption

The source assumes the player characters begin as normal people helping their friend and are drawn into extraordinary circumstances. That assumption matters when converting to D&D: a high-level heroic party could trivialise or tonally distort the intended mystery.

## Confirmed geographical terms

- June Serin: settlement/location where Landry works
- Frontier Lands: broader region Landry travelled to seeking fortune
- Whispered Caverns: mining complex; lowest levels are where the anomalous shifts occur

Exact map, city districts, mine levels and routes are pending PDF extraction.

## Confirmed NPC

- Landry Horn: old friend of the PCs; miner in June Serin; sends coded plea for help

No employer, supervisor, antagonist, other miners, officials or factions are named in the public hook.

## Mystery-integrity requirement

The public hook intentionally presents symptoms, not cause:
- missing memories
- abnormal productivity
- unusually pure ore

The AI DM must not infer a canonical explanation from common fantasy tropes. It cannot decide that mind flayers, enchantment, possession, time distortion, a cursed ore vein, industrial conspiracy or any other hypothesis is true until the source confirms it.

## DMOS adaptation principle

This adventure should become a clue-state graph rather than a linear sequence of exposition. Each clue needs source location, discovery conditions, what it actually proves, what it merely suggests, which NPCs know it, when it can be revealed, and fallback paths if players miss it. Free-text player theories must influence investigation choices without allowing the AI to rewrite the actual mystery answer.
