# Leviathan - Community Intelligence

## High-value actual-play source
Primary creator page comments: https://catshavenolord.itch.io/leviathan

### Verified run profile
- GM: KingDunnad (itch handle)
- Group: 2 players
- Ruleset: Mothership
- Outcome: reported successful/enjoyable session

### Player-response observations
- One player strongly believed there was a literal beast in/near the sun while the other remained sceptical.
- Players theorised they were subjects of an experiment.
- The AI countdown caused immediate panic and hiding.
- A player interrupted the AI's ominous speech by shooting/destroying it.
- An airlock breach plus later messages convinced players a creature was about to enter the station, so they bunkered down.
- One character sealed themself inside a cryopod-equivalent while the other prepared to defend/dispose of the pod.
- The benign central reveal still worked despite players generating much darker theories.

### GM procedure findings
1. **Curated escalation beat pure randomisation.** The GM selected station problems and AI malfunctions in advance so they became progressively worse. They reported this built tension effectively.
2. **Strict timer is optional.** The GM kept a nominal 15-minute timer but delivered messages at better dramatic moments. They suggested intervals could shorten later when players stop moving.
3. **Clues were sufficient.** The GM briefly feared the players would need more clues because they searched aggressively, but the existing set proved sufficient.
4. **Mothership amplified expectation.** The horror ruleset made players expect danger even when the scenario's central threat was benign.
5. **Few rolls were needed.** Investigation dominated play. The GM granted automatic success for sensible actions and rolled when failure had meaningful consequences.
6. **Ignoring problems worked.** Players did not fix every station issue (examples included exploding beakers and a solar-panel problem); they prioritised based on perceived threat.

### Creator response
Luke Simonds explicitly agreed that non-strict message timing can work well. He described his preference for a real-world timer as a technique he enjoys, not a requirement that should override good pacing.

## Independent jam review

The reviewer flagged the Hebrew/zalgo-looking message presentation as difficult to present at the table.

This is independent opinion, not canon.

## DMOS adaptation conclusions

### Adopt
- Allow `timerMode = real-time | paced | accelerating`.
- Default automated DM pacing should be `paced`, because the AI can advance messages when player momentum stalls or tension peaks.
- Permit curated escalation rather than requiring random malfunction selection.
- Track ignored station problems and apply their natural consequences without forcing completion.
- Support AI destruction/interruption and continue the mystery through remaining sources.
- For Mothership, make stress/roll usage event-driven rather than rolling for routine investigation.

### Do not adopt as canon
- the exact player theories from one session
- the GM's cryopod terminology if the source uses a different canonical term
- any improvised room details from that session
- assumption that two players is the official recommended group size; it is only verified evidence that two-player play can work
