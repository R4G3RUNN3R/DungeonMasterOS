# Gold in the Hills - Sources and Rights

## Authoritative research source
- Collection: `Adventure Anthology One`
- Edition: 1st Edition Release 21
- Author: Ray Allen
- Native ruleset: Basic Fantasy Role-Playing Game
- Current indexed text: https://anyflip.com/lbapq/cowt/basic/
- Source party guidance: 2-4 characters, levels 1-3.

The Release 21 anthology licence section designates the anthology text, excluding the Open Game License text itself, as Open Game Content under OGL v1.0a. Artwork and product/product-line branding remain outside that designation as stated by the source.

Production packaging must preserve the complete applicable OGL v1.0a text and Copyright Notice chain. See `../../_legal/ogl-1.0a-packaging.md`.

## Historical standalone source
A standalone `Gold In The Hills` Release 1 is indexed at:
https://www.scribd.com/document/852235443/Basic-Fantasy-Gold-in-the-Hills

It identifies `Gold In The Hills Copyright 2007 Ray Allen`, is distributed under OGL v1.0a, and provides a historical six-page standalone version. Release 21 remains authoritative for current DMOS play if differences are found.

## Community/play sources

### Living 4 Crits actual play, 2015
https://ilive4crits.blogspot.com/2015/07/basic-fantasy-rpg-gold-in-hills-session.html

Family actual play using four randomly generated **level-1** Basic Fantasy characters. The report notes they used maximum starting HP and still found the adventure dangerous enough to joke about it afterward. The GM also improvised `Steel Fisted Goblins`; those are **not source canon** and must never enter the DMOS template as official creatures.

### Felbrigg solo Scarlet Heroes play, 2022
https://felbrigg.blogspot.com/2022/08/

A solo player ran the adventure with a level-1 halfling fighter using Scarlet Heroes conversion. The session followed the entrance guards/pit/T-junction structure. Useful conversion evidence, but Scarlet Heroes is not automatically an approved DMOS ruleset.

### Convention/club use, 2016
https://ilive4crits.blogspot.com/2016/03/?m=0

`Gold in the Hills` was used at a club event with three players and DCC-style 0-level pregens because of OSR mechanical similarity. This is evidence of portability, not native rules support.

## Disposition
`built-in candidate / OGL release package required`

Community play proves the module has actually been run at the low end of its stated band, but balance changes must remain explicit conversion/QA choices rather than silently changing native mode.
