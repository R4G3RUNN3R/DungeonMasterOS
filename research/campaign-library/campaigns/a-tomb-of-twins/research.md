# A Tomb of Twins - Source-Derived Research

## Historical truth
Drezis and Woxis were twin necromancers whose power once terrorized the region. They were defeated centuries ago and their tower was dismantled. Their tomb remains above Dreklow on a sea cliff.

The twins planned to become liches and distrusted one another so thoroughly that each privately planned to poison the other. Their bodies now lie in the Burial Chamber while their soul vials are suspended inside an anti-magic field. The field prevents their lich transformation from completing.

The Priesthood of Zorander maintains this containment. Each month priests enter the tomb, renew/reset its protections and remove dead intruders. A novice is assigned annually to secure the vials; Marzena holds that unpleasant duty during the campaign.

Galduz, an old priest corrupted by prolonged contact with the tomb's magic, wants outsiders to remove the vials so the twins can rise.

Shortly after the most recent monthly ritual, swampkins from the marsh south of Dreklow tunneled into the western side of the tomb. The priesthood has not discovered them yet.

## Dreklow social setup
The village Moot Hall provides rumours and three competing hooks.

### Bogumila
Former cat burglar and current fence. Wants the soul vials as extremely valuable contraband and offers a large cash reward. She knows there are secret doors in Room 2 but not how they open.

### Marzena
Bookish, timid priesthood novice who dislikes the assignment. Wants capable adventurers to retrieve the vials safely and is willing to offer Arcane Academy enchanting services. She knows the tomb's puzzle key is etched over the first internal doorway.

### Galduz
Corrupted elderly priest. Claims the soul vials can grant immortality if brought to him. He actually wants them removed from containment so Drezis and Woxis rise. He knows the tomb in detail but avoids lengthy explanations to reduce the chance his conspiracy is exposed.

## Rumour system
Villagers offer mixed information including:
- the twins may not truly be dead
- the tomb traps ghosts
- stories of secret passages and treasure
- the guardians were created by dividing two volunteers into fourteen faceless constructs
- rumours of eternal life
- true hints about secret doors in Room 2
- false folk procedures for opening the tomb
- false claims about priestly sacrifices
- true reference to powerful healing liquid inside

DMOS stores each rumour with truth state. NPC speech does not automatically update campaign facts.

## Tomb structure
The tomb has 12 keyed locations. Room 2 splits into east and west paths that reconnect at Room 9. Either branch can be explored first.

### Global ghost rule
The tomb traps spirits. Ghosts cannot leave through the outer entrance or Burial Chamber doors/walls. Ghostly insects/rodents occur throughout the complex, and larger ghost encounters may occur during exploration.

Ghost types have different motives: trapped villagers want release, dead wizards want deeper secrets, goblin ghosts may prank, dead priests protect secrets, thieves can be withdrawn but bribable, and rat ghosts simply behave like rats.

A PC who dies inside can continue play as a ghost using the source rules/stat block. This is an important nonstandard failure path and must be supported by DMOS.

## Room logic

### Room 1 - Entrance
Contains visible/telegraphed tripwire attached to a cowbell. Triggering it alerts swampkins in Room 7 and causes them to prepare an ambush. Cryptic glyphs above the inner door are the puzzle sequence used later in Rooms 5 and 8.

### Room 2 - Painted Scenes
Contains hidden east/west doors opened by manipulating disguised gold sconces. Wall carvings show the mirrored solution structure: each necromancer dresses in distinctive hat/robe/shoes and takes a necklace from the central Faceless Figure. Eastern imagery points to moon amulet; western to sun amulet.

### Room 3 - East Treasury
Three trapped chests. Body heat on chest handles activates magnetic/low-gravity effects; cold water from central basin can disable the trap and also lets weapons harm ghosts. Treasures include Drezis's disguise clothing, journal and utility magic items such as sealing wax and endless red string.

Drezis's journal reveals his lich plan, hostility toward Woxis and intent to poison his twin.

### Room 4 - Faceless Banquet
Seven Faceless Figures guard a moon-shaped necklace. They attack entrants not wearing the complete eastern Drezis outfit and stop functioning when the necklace-bearing central figure is killed or stripped of the necklace.

### Room 5 - East Glyph Room
Nine glyph squares reproduce symbols from Room 1. Correct sequence grants safe crossing. Wrong sequence triggers descending/crushing ceiling. Opening/closing exit resets trap.

### Room 6 - West Treasury
Mirrors Room 3 but its trap is already active, leaving a swampkin grunt floating near ceiling. Contains Woxis's disguise outfit, journal, black illumination candle and magic-resistant paste. Water basin disables trap and can empower weapons against ghosts.

The trapped grunt can trade swampkin information for rescue.

### Room 7 - Ruined Banquet
Swampkins have destroyed the original western guardian encounter. Pristina is present. If Room 1's bell rang, six grunts prepare an improvised ambush, including ridiculous faceless-figure disguises. If no alarm, Pristina can be cautious and cooperative.

### Room 8 - West Glyph Room
Same glyph solution principle and crushing-ceiling risk as Room 5. Evidence of a previously crushed swampkin warns of danger.

### Room 9 - Respite
The two paths rejoin. Contains removable glowing orb and a basin of purple liquid that heals wounds once per day. Removed liquid loses potency after a short period. The liquid can also enable weapons to harm ghosts.

### Room 10 - Antechamber
Final bronze doors depict necromantic imagery and have moon/sun-shaped recesses. Correct moon/sun amulets open them safely. Forcing them without the amulets triggers a severe flame trap.

### Room 11 - Burial Chamber
Contains the sarcophagi of Drezis and Woxis and the soul-vial pedestal.

A hidden scroll explains a ritual for static or movable anti-magic protection using the earlier glyphs, blood and liquid from Room 9. The source intentionally allows the referee to tune ritual execution difficulty to table style.

Soul vials are held inside the current anti-magic field. Removing them without moving/recreating the protection allows both necromancers to rise as liches.

If awakened, the twins immediately begin bickering. If nobody intervenes, they escalate into a destructive magical fight, then after a defined period call a truce and decide to conquer the world. Players can exploit their hatred and potentially get one twin to side temporarily against the other.

Emptying a soul vial permanently kills its linked lich. Drinking its contents produces the source life-drain effect on the drinker.

### Room 12 - Swampkin Cave
Swampkins tunneled into the tomb from southern marshes. Warlord Juris, priestess Cestina, brutes and grunts occupy the cave. They have looted Room 7 but failed to solve Room 8 or discover/access other protected areas.

They are not inherently hostile to adventurers and may cooperate for half the treasure, although Juris may double-cross if an opportunity looks profitable.

Their deeper tunnel maze has random navigation outcomes. Drezis's endless red string can create a safe route back to the starting point.

## Swampkin leadership
### Pristina
Kind-hearted priestess of the Great Toad, Cestina's twin. Fascinated by humans. She convinced Juris to raid the tomb and is willing to cooperate for treasure/help.

### Cestina
Meaner twin priestess, communicates with the Great Toad and is skeptical of the raid. She prefers to kill intruding adventurers and go home.

### Juris
New/insecure warlord who wants treasure to prove leadership. Reliant on both priestesses and open to deals or betrayal.

## Final soul-vial outcomes

### Vials remain protected in tomb
The twins remain suspended. The Priesthood can continue monthly containment.

### Vials removed correctly and given to Marzena
She takes them to the Arcane Academy, which recognises the threat and keeps them permanently protected within anti-magic containment.

### Vials given to Bogumila
She sells them to the highest bidder. Without the ritual/containment knowledge accompanying them, the twins rise after the temporary/moved anti-magic protection expires roughly one month later.

### Vials given to Galduz
He deliberately removes them from protection so the twins rise, then kills himself.

### PCs keep vials
They must renew anti-magic protection monthly or create another effective containment solution. Failure causes future lich awakening.

### Vials emptied
The corresponding lich is permanently destroyed at the cost of the valuable vial contents/item.

### Both liches leave the tomb
They attack Dreklow, destroy the Priesthood and Sanctum, enslave villagers, rebuild their tower and grow a regional undead army with eventual world-conquest ambitions.

This consequence must persist outside the one-shot. It is a campaign-state failure/continuation seed, not a reset.
