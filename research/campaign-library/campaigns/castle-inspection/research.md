# Castle Inspection - Source-derived research

This file contains only facts supported by the current source set. Missing details are marked pending rather than invented.

## Core premise

The player characters are undead heroes who work for the lich Radagast. Radagast summons them during an unannounced visit by Imperial Insurance Inspector Ignatius the Intransigent. Radagast is overwhelmed with paperwork and believes another disastrous inspection will increase the castle's insurance cost dramatically.

The castle is already in severe disorder when the inspection begins. Public creator descriptions confirm fire, loose monsters, unraveling rituals, a haunted library, crying monster babies in the cellar, and free-roaming goats. Ignatius actively searches for violations. The party must repair, conceal, redirect, deceive, or otherwise control problems before he discovers them.

The adventure was written around the 2025 contest theme `Never Split the Party`, deliberately encouraging simultaneous problem-solving and potentially dividing the party across crises.

## Confirmed named characters

- Radagast: lich and owner/master of the castle; employer of the undead heroes; trapped behind a large quantity of paperwork during the emergency.
- Ignatius the Intransigent: Imperial Insurance Inspector; strict rules-focused inspector who looks for violations that can raise the policy cost.
- Butler Bones: helper Radagast says he has assigned to assist the heroes.
- Unnamed skeleton from the previous inspection: assigned the prior year, suffered a breakdown and quit according to Radagast's creator-published introduction.

## Pressure model implied by the source

Confirmed presentation establishes three concurrent pressures suitable for later formal modelling:

1. **Inspector exposure**: Ignatius is looking for contract violations.
2. **Crisis escalation**: multiple problems are already active and may worsen if left unattended.
3. **Time/attention division**: the adventure explicitly tells the group to split up and fix problems quickly.

A DMOS implementation should eventually track these as separate state variables, but exact thresholds must come from the full adventure or be clearly labelled as DMOS adaptation rules.

## Likely play modes, separated from canon

The source's intended mode is simultaneous crisis resolution and party splitting. Community feedback identified two alternate presentation modes that could improve turn participation in an AI interface:

- group preparation before Ignatius starts a room-by-room tour
- an NPC escort who moves Ignatius slowly while all PCs remain available to solve crises

These are not hard source canon and belong in DMOS adaptation design unless the complete PDF explicitly supports them.
